German Confederation

The German Confederation (German: Deutscher Bund) was an association of 39 predominantly German-speaking sovereign states in Central Europe, created by the Congress of Vienna in 1815 as a replacement for the former Holy Roman Empire.

Dominated politically by the Austrian Empire and the Kingdom of Prussia, the Confederation operated through a Federal Convention (Bundesversammlung) in Frankfurt, characterized by bureaucratic centralization, strict censorship under the Carlsbad Decrees, and police oversight.

State administration emphasized monistic public law, raison d'etat, and administrative obedience over local autonomous custom.
